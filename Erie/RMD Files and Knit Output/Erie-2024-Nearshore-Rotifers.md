## Summer NS-OS Rotifer Genera Average Biomass Barplot

![Figure 1A. Summer Rotifer genera nearshore (NS) vs. offshore (OS) average biomass (mg dry wt per m^3^). Error bars represent standard errors of the means. Taxa groups are identified in legend by color.](Erie-2024-Nearshore-Rotifers_files/figure-docx/Summer NS-OS Rotifer Genera Average Biomass Barplot-1.png)

Figure 1A shows the volumetric biomass of the 13 most commonly
encountered taxa between the nearshore and offshore samples for Lake Erie in
August 2024. With several exceptions, most rotifer taxa exhibited higher
average biomass values in the offshore samples, suggesting that these
waters supported higher densities and perhaps larger taxa compared to
shallower habitats. Several exceptions to this were *Trichocerca*, which had
a higher biomass in the nearshore vs. offshore, and can be attributed to
the higher nutrient concentrations found in nearshore waters supporting
a greater density of these eutrophic-loving taxa (Gannon and Stemberger
1978; Sládecek, 1983). *Synchaeta* and *Ploesoma* showed a 
slight preference for the nearshore waters while *Conochiloides* showed a 
preference for the offshore waters. The remaining taxa *Polyarthra*, 
*Ascomorpha*, *Brachionus*, *Keratella*, etc, exhibited very similar biomass 
values between near- and offshore waters. These insights suggest that despite 
potentially higher food concentrations in nearshore habitats, only several taxa 
gain a clear advantage in these conditions.

## Rotifer Genera Summer Nearshore-Offshore Density Barplot

![Figure 1B. Summer Rotifer genera nearshore (NS) vs. offshore (OS) average density (# per m^3^). Error bars represent standard errors of the means. Taxa groups are identified in legend by color.](Erie-2024-Nearshore-Rotifers_files/figure-docx/Summer NS-OS Rotifer Genera Average Density Barplot-1.png)

Figure 1B shows the density of the 13 most commonly encountered 
taxa between the nearshore and offshore samples for Lake Erie in
August 2024. With several exceptions, most rotifer taxa exhibited higher
average density values in the nearshore samples, which is in contrast to 
average biomass values, indicating that smaller specimens or species are found 
in shallower habitats. Several taxa (*Trichocerca*, *Polyarthra*, *Synchaeta*, 
*Ascomorpha*) showed higher densities in the nearshore vs. offshore waters. 
*Conochiloides* was the only taxa that showed a clear preference for the 
offshore waters. The remaining taxa *Brachionus*, *Keratella*, *Kellicottia*, 
etc, exhibited very similar density values between near- and offshore waters. 
These insights suggest that despite potentially higher food concentrations in 
nearshore habitats, only several taxa gain an advantage in these conditions. 
Additionally, the contrasting findings that densities for several taxa are 
higher in the nearshore while biomass is similar or higher in the offshore, 
indicates there is a different rotifer community between the sample types.

## Rotifer Summer Genera Station Total Biomass Barplot

![Figure 5A. Summer Rotifer genera station total biomass (mg dry wt per m^3^) for the the 12 CSMI nearshore sites ordered from east to west (7 northshore, 5 south), and the 20 offshore sites ordered from east to west (4 east basin, 10 central, 6 west).](Erie-2024-Nearshore-Rotifers_files/figure-docx/NS-OS Summer Rotifer Genera Station Biomass-1.png)

Figure 5A represents the individual station total rotifer biomass for the 13 
most common taxa collected during the Lake Erie August 2024 survey. The stations 
are separated by the nearshore (NS) and offshore (OS) samples ordered from east 
to west. The first 7 listed nearshore samples were collected from the northshore 
of Lake Erie and show a lower overall biomass compared with the 5 subsequent 
stations collected from the southshore of the lake. That biomass disparity is 
largely driven by higher *Asplanchna* values in several southshore samples. 
Station ER936n shows a particularly diverse community compared with other 
stations, and is close in proximity to Van Buren Bay close to Dunkirk, NY. The 
offshore samples are separated by their basins (4 east, 10 central, 6 west), 
which are distinct due to the large change in bathymetric depths between basins,
resulting in very different ecological implications for the zooplankton 
communities. The eastern basin is the deepest and most similar to the Lake 
Ontario rotifer community, and with the exception of ER15M which is missing the 
large *Asplanchna* biomass, those 4 samples are similar. The first 3 central 
samples are similar with relatively low overall biomass with the addition of 
more *Polyarthra*. With the exception of ER42 which shows a massive *Asplancha* 
biomass, the remaining central stations exhibit a similar community, with more 
*Polyarthra* and *Trichocera* biomass moving westward. The western basin is 
historically the most diverse community in the Great Lakes, with very shallow 
and eutrophic waters that support a unique assemblage of rotifers. A higher 
proportion of *Trichocera*, *Polyarthra*, and *Brachionus* is indication of 
greater nutrient availability, and therefore a more diverse set of food 
resources compared with other localities. It should be noted that the despite 
these resources, the western basin often exhibits the lowest biomass and density 
of rotifers, which is impacted by the shallow waters (~6 meters), discharge from
the Detroit River and Maumee River, and higher potential of predation by 
larger zooplankton and larval fish.
